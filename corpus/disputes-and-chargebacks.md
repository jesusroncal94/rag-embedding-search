# Disputes and Chargebacks

## Raising a dispute

A customer can dispute a card transaction in the app up to 120 days after the transaction date. The dispute form asks for the reason code, whether the customer contacted the merchant first, and any supporting evidence such as an order confirmation or a cancellation email.

Disputes for transactions the customer authorised but is unhappy with, such as a service not delivered, require proof that the merchant was contacted and did not resolve the issue. Disputes for unauthorised transactions do not require contacting the merchant.

## Provisional credit

For unauthorised transaction disputes, a provisional credit is applied to the account within ten business days of the case being opened. The provisional credit is reversed if the investigation concludes the transaction was authorised by the customer.

Provisional credit is not applied to disputes about merchandise quality or services not received, because those cases are resolved directly with the merchant under the scheme rules.

## Investigation timeline

The scheme allows the merchant bank 45 days to respond to a chargeback. The customer is informed of the outcome in the app and by email. If the merchant bank contests the chargeback, the case moves to pre-arbitration and can take up to 90 days in total.

## Liability for unauthorised payments

Where a payment was not authorised by the customer and the customer did not act fraudulently or with gross negligence, the customer is liable for a maximum of 50 EUR of the loss. Once the customer has reported the loss, theft or misuse of the card, the customer bears no further loss at all.

Sharing a PIN or a one-time passcode with a third party, including someone claiming to be a bank employee, is treated as gross negligence.
